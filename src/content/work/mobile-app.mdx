## The Problem

A small startup had an AI backend they were confident in and a web prototype that validated the core concept. They needed a real mobile app, fast, and had one designer slot available.

The product's value was in the quality of the AI conversation — the design job was to get out of the way and make that conversation feel effortless on a phone.

## Approach

Started with competitive analysis focused not on AI apps — most of them are bad — but on the best conversation apps generally. Signal. iMessage. Telegram. What makes a message thread feel alive and responsive? What makes input feel low-friction?

Spent the first week on the conversation thread UI exclusively, because everything else — navigation, settings, history — is scaffolding. If the core conversation experience wasn't right, nothing else would matter.

## The Work

**Conversation thread.** Designed a clear visual hierarchy between user messages and AI responses, with distinct but not heavy styling. Typing indicators, streaming text rendering, and citation chips for grounded responses. Spent significant time on the message input area — the balance between a compact composer and one that doesn't feel cramped when typing long prompts.

**Navigation model.** Chose a simple two-level structure: conversation list → conversation. Resisted adding tabs or a drawer. When the product does one thing well, the navigation should reflect that.

**Gesture system.** Swipe-to-archive on conversations, long-press context menus, pull-to-refresh on history. All standard iOS patterns, deliberately — the app should feel native immediately, not like a web app wrapped in a shell.

**Onboarding.** Three screens max. Show the product immediately. This was a strong conviction from the founder and it was right.

## Results

Launched on schedule to the App Store. 4.7-star rating in the first two weeks with over 2,000 downloads in the first seven days. The founder described the design as "the thing that made people believe the product was real."
