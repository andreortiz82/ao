## The Problem

Clinical research sites get paid per visit activity. A lab draw here, a questionnaire there, a physical exam — each one has a negotiated rate in the study budget. The problem: tracking which activities happened in which visit, then matching them to the right line item in a budget, was entirely manual.

Sites were using spreadsheets. Finance coordinators were cross-referencing paper visit logs, budget documents, and invoicing templates by hand. The result was predictable: missed billable activities, double-billed items, invoices that didn't match the protocol, and weeks of back-and-forth with sponsors to resolve discrepancies.

The financial risk was real. Underbilling meant leaving money on the table. Overbilling meant sponsor audits and relationship damage. Either way, the site lost.

## The Approach

We interviewed finance coordinators and site managers across eight sites. The recurring theme: everyone knew the data was in the system, but no one could get it out in a form they could actually use for billing.

The source of truth existed. The problem was translation — taking completed visit data and turning it into a reconciled, defensible invoice. That translation step was where errors crept in, where time disappeared, and where disputes originated.

If we could automate the translation — map completed activities directly to budget line items, generate an invoice draft automatically, and surface discrepancies before they became disputes — we could solve the problem at the source.

## Key Decisions

**Activity-to-budget mapping.** Every visit activity in the system is mapped to a budget line item at setup. When a coordinator marks an activity complete, the system knows what it's worth and who to bill.

**Matrix view.** The budget matrix gives finance teams a complete picture — every activity, every visit, completion status, billing status — in a single view. No spreadsheet required.

**Automated invoice drafts.** At the end of a billing cycle, the system generates an invoice draft populated from completed activities. The coordinator reviews, adjusts if needed, and submits. The audit trail is automatic.
