## The Problem

Clinical research visits aren't flexible. Every participant visit happens within a defined protocol window — miss it, and you have a schedule deviation. Enough deviations and the study data is compromised. Enough compromised data and the study fails.

The sites managing these schedules were doing it in spreadsheets. Manually calculating visit windows from enrollment dates. Tracking deviations in separate logs. Cross-referencing protocol amendments by hand. It worked — barely — until it didn't.

The cost wasn't just operational. A missed visit window that creates a protocol deviation can trigger a regulatory finding. In clinical research, that's existential.

## The Approach

We spent the first six weeks embedded with clinical coordinators at three different sites. The patterns were immediate and consistent: everyone had a personal system for tracking visits, no two systems looked alike, and the cognitive load of managing a single participant across a multi-visit study was enormous.

The insight that shaped everything: the protocol schedule is fixed and knowable. Every visit date, every window, every deadline can be calculated deterministically from a single anchor — the enrollment date. The coordinators weren't doing complex work. They were doing repetitive arithmetic, by hand, under pressure.

If we could make that arithmetic invisible, we could give them their attention back.

## Key Decisions

**Protocol-aware scheduling engine.** The system ingests a study protocol and generates a complete visit calendar for every enrolled participant. No manual date math. No spreadsheet formulas. The schedule is always current, always correct, always tied to the protocol version in effect.

**Deviation risk surfacing.** Rather than flagging deviations after they happen, the system surfaces upcoming windows that are at risk — too close to a holiday, conflicting with another participant, outside a site's operating hours. Coordinators can act before a deviation occurs.

**Branching protocol support.** Some studies have conditional visits — if a participant responds a certain way, they go down a different branch. The node map makes branching logic visible and navigable, even for complex multi-arm studies.
