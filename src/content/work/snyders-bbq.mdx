## The Problem

A new BBQ restaurant opening in Austin needed more than a logo. The owner had a strong point of view about the food — wood-fired, unhurried, no shortcuts — but the brand didn't exist yet. Everything had to be created from nothing, on a tight timeline before opening.

The risk with BBQ branding is defaulting to the obvious: distressed fonts, longhorn skulls, fake vintage. None of that reflects what this place actually is.

## Approach

Spent time with the owner first — not to gather a brief, but to understand the obsession. What pitmasters does he respect? What does he think is wrong with how most people approach brisket? The answers to those questions became the brief.

The reference territory I arrived at: mid-century Texas roadside, craft signage, hand-painted lettering traditions. Not nostalgic cosplay — a real connection to the visual culture of the craft.

## The Work

Designed the wordmark around a custom lettering system — hand-drawn, then refined digitally. The goal was something that looked like it had been painted on a cinderblock wall, but still worked at 16px on a website.

Built out a full identity system: primary and secondary wordmarks, a badge lockup for packaging, a restricted color palette (smoke black, bone white, burnt orange), and a custom pattern derived from wood grain cross-sections.

Delivered a print-ready menu system, signage specs, t-shirt and hat artwork, butcher paper bag designs, and a brand standards document the owner could hand to vendors.

## Results

The identity has been in production since opening. The owner reports that customers regularly comment on the brand, and the t-shirts sell consistently — which for a small restaurant is a strong signal the brand is resonating beyond just "here's where you ate."
